### Class Description

This course provides students with a broad understanding of modern software
testing and quality assurance. Although it will cover testing theory, the
emphasis is on providing practical skills in software testing currently used in
industry. To that end, it will cover: manual and automated tests, test-driven
development, performance testing, and model checking.

### Class Organization

This class strives to give you hands-on experience on a broad set of software
QA tools and methodologies.  As such, it is organized around 10 exercises
focused on 10 different techniques, one exercise per week.  Each exercise is
prefaced by a lecture providing you with the conceptual underpinnings of the
technique and is bookended by a short quiz that assesses your conceptual
understanding and application skills.

At the end of the semester, there will be a final exam that assesses your
holistic conceptual understanding, as well as a final project that asks you to
apply all the skills you have learned on a piece of software.

Please refer to the [course schedule page](schedule.md).  

## Course Components

### Exercises

Exercises are [formative
assessments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formative_assessment) --- meaning
that there main purpose is to improve your learning.  As such, you are
encouraged to use the resources available to you at the fullest to complete
them.  This includes asking for help from your instructor, your classmates, and
even generative AI.  I will leave it to you to make judicious use of these
resources so that they contribute rather than harm your learning.  As a general
rule, you will achieve this by attempting to complete the assignment without
any help and asking targeted questions only when you encounter a road block ---
this applies regardless of whether you are soliciting help from a human or an
AI.

Exercises are submitted on GradeScope and, in most cases, an autograder will
provide you with immediate feedback on (part of) your score.  You are allowed
to submit as many times as you wish until the due date --- only your most
recent submission will be graded.

### Quizzes

You can expect 6 quizzes to happen throughout the semester, according to the
schedule.  The quizzes are open book, but limited only to hard copies --- no
electronic devices are allowed other than your laptop showing the quiz on
GradeScope at all times.  That means by extension that searching the web and
asking an AI is prohibited.  All questions in the quiz can be answered after
reviewing the corresponding lecture and exercise.

Quizzes will be given at the start of the given class and you need to be on
time and in the classroom when the quiz starts for your submission to be
accepted.  TopHat attendance will be taken to check your presence in the
classroom.

### Final Exam and Final Project

The final exam and project are [summative
assessments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summative_assessment) --- meaning
that their main purposes is to measure your competency at the end of the
semester.  As such, the use of generative AI for either is strictly prohibited.
You should have learned the material by now and be able to demonstrate your
competency without the help of AI.

The final exam is open book but again only limited to hard copies.  The
difficulty level is largely comparable to the quizzes but in some cases you may
be asked to compare and analyze multiple QA techniques at once with your
accumulated knowledge.

The final project is a group project done in groups of two (no more than two
please).  If you have reason to work alone (for example, your work schedule
makes collaboration difficult), then you may do so.  You will work on it
throughout the semester, applying new QA techniques incrementally as you
acquire them through exercises.  The work that you do for the project must stay
within the group and sharing it outside the group is a violation of academic
integrity.

Since you will be working closely with your partner throughout the semester, I
encourage you to sit next to your partner once groups are formed at the
beginning of the semester.  I expect equal contribution to the project from
both partners and you will be self reporting the division of work.  The self
reported division of work must be well supported by the git commit history of
your GitHub repository for the project.  

Please note that group work is not necessarily less work --- it is a different
type of work.  You should be spending as much time communicating and planning
as you would have otherwise spent coding.  In the process, hopefully you can
learn how to collaborate and perhaps come to enjoy working together.  You can
use Teams to communicate and VSCode live sharing and frequent git repository
synchronization for synchronous / asynchronous collaboration.

Please submit the Partnership Contract on GradeScope, regardless of whether you
will be working in a group or alone.  If you are working in a group, your
expectations as a group member are outlined clearly on the contract.  By
signing it, you are agreeing to its terms so please read carefully.

### Assignment submission

Most assignments will be done in a GitHub Classroom repository.  Th The
repository can be submitted in its entirety to GradeScope.  Make sure you
commit and push your changes before submitting.  Please keep your repository
**private** at all times to prevent plagiarism.

For the final project especially, you are asked to commit early and often to
the repository as commit history will be used as the ground truth for proof of
work (especially as it relates to a group project).  Also, frequent commits
help avoid version conflicts when working on the same files as your partner.
If any disputes from groups arise, I will assume that GitHub is the "ground
truth".  

All exercises and the final project are expected to be submitted on time.  Late
exercises and projects will receive a -10 percentage point penalty per day.
Late submissions are allowed only when the instructor is notified ahead of time
before the due date.

### Programming Language Selection

For code submissions, the class will use Java 11 overwhelmingly and a little
bit of C and TypeScript where appropriate.  Please do not worry if you do not
know TypeScript --- you will learn just enough to do Playwright web application
testing.

### Participating in Class

Questions and comments are invited and strongly encouraged.  If you have a view
point or experience that may enrich the class, please jump in!  There are
several ways you can participate:

1. In-class communication

   If you are in-person and you have a question or comment, just interrupt me
and interject.  You don't even need to raise your hand.

2. Out-of-class communication

   **We will make use of Microsoft Teams for all out-of-class communication**.
Please install Teams on all your devices so that you get real time
notifications and we can carry on real time conversations as the need arises.

   As the semester progresses, each exercise that you work on will gain its own
Teams channel.  Please post all exercise related questions there.  This way,
the entire classroom can benefit from your question.  Please do not be shy ---
if you have a questions, chances are that there are ten more classmates with
the same question who will be thanking you.  And sometimes one of your
classmates has a better answer than the instructor (has happened on more than
one occasion).  It is perfectly okay to show the part of your work relevant to
the question on the posting as exercises are for learning.  If you DM me, I
will most likely gently steer you to posting on the channel.

   You can also send a private message to your instructor using the Teams chat
feature, if you want a private conversation.  This may be a question where you
need to share your work on your final project that cannot be posted on a
channel for academic integrity reasons.  Or, you may just want strike a
personal conversation with your instructor about something of interest, related
to the class or not.

   Whether you post or chat with me, if you are asking a code related question,
please submit the version of the code in question to GradeScope before asking.
That way, your instructor can reproduce your bug and give you a faster more
accurate answer.  

3. Email / Canvas messages

   Please use Teams for all communications.  By using Teams chat instead of
email, all previous conversations are on the same window so it is much easier
to keep track of context.  Also, often I will be able to respond immediately on
Teams and carry on a real time conversation.  Teams even allows us to transition
to video calls or screen-sharing if needed.  

### In-Person Attendance

### Academic Integrity Statement

Cheating/plagiarism will _not_ be tolerated. Students suspected of violating
the [SCI Academic Integrity
Policy](https://www.sci.pitt.edu/student-resources/policies/academic-integrity-policy)
will receive a minimum sanction of a zero score for the exam or assignment.

Some guidelines:

_1. For exercises, you are allowed (and encouraged) to discuss each other's
work and get assistance from generative AI.  All I ask is for you to be
judicious in getting help so that the help acts as a bridge rather than a
barrier to learning.

_2. For the final project, viewing work done by your classmates (with the
exception of your partner) or from a 3rd source pertaining to the assignment is
considered cheating.  Aiding this activity is also considered cheating.  
Any assistance from generative AI is considered cheating as well.

_3. For the quizzes and final exam, you may view hard copy materials that you
bring with you (books, printed material, handwritten material) but you may not
use any electronic devices except your laptop to take the quiz on GradeScope.
Any use of the web or generative AI to access information is prohibited.
